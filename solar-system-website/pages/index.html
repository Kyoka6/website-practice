Celestial body

The Sun

The Sun is the star at the center of our solar system. It creates the energy that supports life on Earth and shapes the environment of every planet in the system.

  • It is a yellow dwarf star.
  • It is about 4.6 billion years old.
  • It produces energy through nuclear fusion.
Type
G-type main-sequence star
Diameter
About 1.39 million km

Celestial body

Mercury

Mercury is the closest planet to the Sun and has extreme temperature swings because it lacks a meaningful atmosphere.

  • It is the smallest planet in the solar system.
  • It has a heavily cratered surface.
  • It completes a year in just 88 Earth days.
Type
Rocky terrestrial planet
Moons
0

Celestial body

Venus

Venus is wrapped in thick clouds and is the hottest planet in our solar system due to its dense greenhouse atmosphere.

  • It spins very slowly in the opposite direction.
  • Its surface is hidden beneath clouds.
  • It is often called Earth's twin because of its similar size.
Type
Rocky terrestrial planet
Moons
0

Celestial body

Earth

Earth is the only known planet where life thrives. Oceans, air, and climate systems make it uniquely suited to support diverse life.

  • About 71% of its surface is covered by oceans.
  • It has one natural moon.
  • Its atmosphere protects life from harmful radiation.
Type
Rocky terrestrial planet
Moons
1

Celestial body

Mars

Mars is a cold, dusty world with the tallest volcano and deepest canyon in the solar system.

  • It has polar ice caps.
  • Its red color comes from iron oxide.
  • It has two small moons: Phobos and Deimos.
Type
Rocky terrestrial planet
Moons
2

Celestial body

Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet and a stormy world known for the Great Red Spot, a giant anticyclonic storm.

  • It is a gas giant.
  • It has many moons, including Europa and Io.
  • It has a faint ring system.
Type
Gas giant
Moons
95+

Celestial body

Saturn

Saturn is famous for its bright rings, which are made mostly of ice and rock fragments.

  • It is the second-largest planet.
  • Its rings extend far beyond the planet itself.
  • It is less dense than water.
Type
Gas giant
Moons
146+

Celestial body

Uranus

Uranus is an ice giant that rotates on its side, giving it an unusual seasonal pattern.

  • Its blue-green color comes from methane in its atmosphere.
  • It has a faint ring system.
  • It is the coldest planet in the solar system.
Type
Ice giant
Moons
27

Celestial body

Neptune

Neptune is the farthest planet from the Sun and is known for its wild storms and fast winds.

  • It is an ice giant.
  • It has a dark storm called the Great Dark Spot.
  • It has a faint ring system.
Type
Ice giant
Moons
14

Celestial body

Moon

Earth's Moon is the closest celestial body to our planet and helps shape ocean tides and the rhythm of the night sky.

  • It is Earth's only natural satellite.
  • It has no atmosphere to hold heat.
  • Its phases repeat every 29.5 days.
Type
Natural satellite
Distance
About 384,400 km from Earth
